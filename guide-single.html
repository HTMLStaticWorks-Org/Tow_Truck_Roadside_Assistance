Highway Safety

What to do immediately after a highway tire blowout

Sarah Jenkins • Lead Dispatch Commander

Oct 12, 2026 • 5 min read

A sudden tire blowout at 65 mph creates loud noise and strong steering pull, tempting many drivers to stomp on the brakes. That instinct is the most dangerous reaction you can have on an open expressway.

Control the Vehicle First

Grip the steering wheel firmly with both hands at 9 and 3 o'clock, keep your foot off the brake pedal, and briefly maintain light throttle for one or two seconds to stabilize forward momentum while you signal right.

Let engine drag slow the vehicle naturally as you glide onto the widest flat stretch of the right shoulder—away from blind curves or narrow bridge railings.

Secure Your Scene Before Calling Dispatch

Turn on your hazard flashers immediately, angle your front wheels away from live traffic lanes, and remain buckled inside the cabin if high-speed cars are passing close to your driver-side door. Share your GPS pin in the TowGuard portal so our amber-lit safety rig can block the shoulder behind you.

Key Roadside Safety Takeaways

  • Never slam the brakes during a blowout — coast smoothly onto the right shoulder.
  • Do not attempt a driver-side tire change next to fast-moving highway lanes.
  • Request a TowGuard roadside unit with live GPS pin sharing for faster, protected service.
